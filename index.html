01
The Fuel Shock

Since 2022, Australia faced consistent fuel price pressures. Price levels were highly variable among different states, there was increasing volatility of cycle patterns in eastern cities, and transport prices made up a greater proportion of people’s income.

Vis 1 · Choropleth Map

Fuel Price Across Australian States

Northern Territory tops the list of the most expensive prices for petrol at 212.9 c/L, which is greater than 15% than that of the second most expensive state (QLD, 187.5 c/L). This is because of remoteness and high transportation cost. The cheapest state is SA with price at 180.4 c/L

Vis 2 · Heatmap + Marginal Histograms

Fuel Price Cycle Patterns by City

Perth has a highly compressed cycle of seven days, which is very predictable and strictly controlled. Cities in the east, such as Melbourne, Sydney, and Brisbane, have cycles of 33-41 days, which means that consumers must wait much longer before knowing whether the prices will go up or down.

Vis 3 · Faceted Area + Line Chart

Transport Affordability & Fuel Cost Share Over Time

The cost of transport in Melbourne increased by 17% to 17.9% of its income between 2019 and 2023 while that for Geelong followed a similar pattern although on a lower level. The red dashed line representing the share of fuel in the total cost of transport remains almost constant at 16-21%.

Vis 4 · Annotated Bar Chart

State-by-State Fuel Price Ranking

It is evident that countries are very tightly grouped together in the range 183–188 c/L, indicating that national supply characteristics take precedence over state-related features; however, the exception to this is NT, as it diverges distinctly from this cluster of countries.

02
Government Response

State and federal governments rolled out a range of measures such as fuel management, price controls, tax cuts, and even free or subsidized transport services. Victoria, in particular, implemented the strongest policy, which led to experiments with free public transport in 2026.

Vis 5 · Annotated Scatter Timeline

Policy & Shock Events: 2022 – 2026

The escalation of policies is obvious here: Victoria went from passive monitoring (2023) to active price regulation (2025) and free public transport (2026). The cost-of-living crisis of 2022 and the international oil price fluctuations are evident in the graph as blue circles, which indicate that government policies were reactive rather than proactive.

Vis 6 · Proportional Symbol Map

Geographic Distribution of Policy Interventions

The state of Victoria dominates the number of policy events; its capital cities ensure that transport policy is both urgent and solvable. The impact of national external events on policy is illustrated by Australia's central point geographically. There were no interventions recorded in TAS or NSW in this time period.

Vis 7 · Grouped Bar Chart

Policy Spending by State & Program Classification

The Queensland “50 cent for public transport program” classified as being “Partly” dedicated for transport relief was funded at a cost of $1 billion, which was substantially more than all other programs in the states. The federal government, on the other hand, spent its $195 million on totally dedicated programs.

Vis 8 · Multi-Line Chart

Policy Intensity Over Time by State

The most intense policy score (5/5) is retained by Victoria and Queensland up to 2025–2026. The Federal Government achieved a peak of 5 in 2022, after which it fell due to a reduction in the fuel excise. The state of Western Australia scores 4 consistently owing to the FuelWatch initiative.

03
Public Transport Recovery

Victoria’s transport system bounced back tremendously during 2025. Metro rail and tram systems spearheaded this revival, with peak periods being recorded in March and May, implying the possible resumption of commuting by the general population due to rising costs in privately owned automobiles.

Vis 9 · Multi-Line Chart

Monthly Patronage by Transport Mode (2025)

Metro Train led the way with a peak number of 17-18 million rides in March and May. Tram service matched train performance closely. Bus service was consistently trailing train and tram service. The drop off in June-July is understandable because it coincides with holiday time.

Vis 10 · Stacked Area Chart

Transport Composition — Stacked Monthly View

The stacked area graph demonstrates the domination of metropolitan services. Metro Train and Tram make up around 65% of total monthly trips. Regional services make up a very slim base. The patronage of the entire network is more than 45 million monthly trips.

Vis 11 · Slope / Line Chart

Ridership Snapshot: Key Months

In comparison with January, March and May demonstrate strong mid-year recovery. All modes apart from Regional Coach display better performance in terms of passengers in March and May compared to January, indicating the effect of increased gasoline prices.

Vis 12 · Bubble Chart

Annual Passenger Volume by Mode

Metro Train ferried 185 million passengers in 2025, surpassing the number of passengers carried by Metro Tram (155 million) and Metro Bus (111 million) combined. The mode with the lowest value is Regional Coach (1.4 million). The values have been labeled appropriately.

04
Behaviour Change

Ultimate query: Did the rising cost of fuel and policy action actually change the travel habits of Australia? In this part, the relationship between the price of fuel and the train usage is analyzed along with the shifting mode share prior to and following policy implementation.

Vis 13 · Connected Scatter Plot

Fuel Price vs Train Ridership — Monthly Path (2025)

These connected scatter plots illustrate the monthly journey in terms of fuel price / ridership. The higher price months of September and October generally coincide with higher ridership levels, which conforms to the substitution effect. March is an outlier in terms of the high level of ridership in relation to moderate prices.

Vis 14 · Normalised Stacked Bar

Mode Share: Before vs After Policy

Car mode share dropped by 9 percentage points to 49%. The share of train increased by 6 percentage points (22% to 28%) while that of tram increased by 2 pp, and bus by 1 percentage point. It’s a significant shift in mode share as a result of the effect of fuel cost rise and subsidized transport.

Vis 15 · Dual-Axis Annotated Chart

Fuel Price, Ridership & Policy Moments — Summary

This is depicted in the chart where fuel prices (in red) and train ridership (in blue) are presented against time along with marked vertical lines for important events. The introduction of the Fuel Excise Cut in August saw a slight decrease in prices; the Fair Fuel Plan in October saw ridership staying high despite cooler weather.
