Impact of Fuel Prices and Transport Relief on Public Transport Usage in Australia

Section 1: The Fuel Shock

Vis 1 — Fuel Price Map

Vis 2 — Fuel Price Cycle Patterns

Australian cities show very different fuel pricing cycles. WA is highly predictable with short cycles, while eastern states experience longer and more volatile pricing patterns.

Vis 3 — Transport Affordability Distribution (Melbourne vs Geelong)

This faceted density plot compares how transport affordability is distributed over time between Melbourne and Geelong. Melbourne consistently shows higher transport cost pressure as a percentage of income, while Geelong remains lower but gradually converges upward. Fuel share contributes a stable but significant portion of total transport costs.

Vis 4 — Most Expensive States (Ranked)

The graph below displays a comparison of the fuel prices for various states in Australia by utilizing the combination of the layered histogram and the cumulative histogram. This particular representation helps the audience to recognize that there are two factors to be considered: the number of states in a certain price category and the accumulation of states with increasing prices. It is worth emphasizing that fuel prices are not equally distributed in all regions; therefore, fuel prices group into certain bands due to their geographical location and policies implemented in them.

Section 2: Government Response

Vis 5 — Policy Timeline

The above visualization depicts the increasing pressures faced by the people of Australia due to rising costs of living associated with fuel and the government's policy approach to this problem. Early responses involved surveillance of the fuel supply and transparency of prices; more recent steps have seen the introduction of price control mechanisms, like daily ceilings on fuel prices. Policy makers in Victoria moved their attention to transport issues in 2026 by providing subsidies for public transport.

Vis 6 — Policy Coverage Map

This map highlights the spatial distribution of governmental response to the fuel price volatility in Victoria, as well as illustrating the national-level shock events that affect the whole country.

  • Victoria exhibits heavy government intervention (price cap, transparency, public transport assistance)
  • External shocks include national events (fuel volatility, cost of living)
  • Size of circle reflects level of policy implementation; escalation is observed in 2025-2026

    The grouped bar graph facilitates the comparison of expenditure on different policies among different states without compromising on the categorization aspect of each type of expenditure.     Vis 8 — Policy Intensity (Faceted)

    Based on the results from the faceted visualization, there is evidence indicating that there was a variation in the actions taken by the Australian government to address issues related to affordable fuel. Some states like Victoria and Queensland used more aggressive approaches compared to other states, which used less severe and mainly monitoring approaches. The variations indicate the level of priority placed in dealing with fuel prices depending on economic, transportation, and living-cost factors.

    Section 3: Public Transport Recovery

    Vis 9 — Multi-Line Transport Usage

    There was notable recovery of usage in the public transport sector for the entire year of 2025, with the metro trains leading in terms of passenger numbers. Usage of trams and metro buses was also notable, whereas regional services were less prominent. As can be seen from the graph below, public transport played a major role in Victoria's transportation network, especially after times of volatile fuel costs.

    Vis 10 — Stacked Area Transport Composition

    The composition of Victoria's public transport network remained relatively stable throughout 2025. Metropolitan train services consistently represented the largest share of passengers, while trams and buses formed the second-largest transport modes. Regional services contributed a smaller proportion of overall trips but maintained steady usage throughout the year.

    Vis 11 — Ridership Growth Snapshot

    Passenger volumes increased substantially after the beginning of 2025, particularly for metropolitan train and tram services. Most transport modes maintained higher passenger numbers throughout the year compared with January levels, suggesting continued recovery and sustained demand for public transport.

    Vis 12 — Mode Share Bubble Chart

    Metropolitan train services carried substantially more passengers than any other transport mode during 2025. Tram and metropolitan bus services formed the second largest categories, while regional transport services accounted for a considerably smaller share of total patronage. This highlights the central role of metropolitan rail in Victoria's transport network.
